About · Civil Services Examination

The exam, explained

One examination, three stages, a little over two thousand marks and roughly a thousand posts. Everything below — pattern, papers, vacancies, cut-offs, eligibility — is drawn from UPSC's own notices, with the numbers current as of November 2026.

3 stages Prelims → Mains → Interview
2,025 marks that decide your rank
1,016 vacancies notified, CSE 2026
180 IAS seats, unchanged since 2015
Right now

CSE 2026 is at the interview stage

Mains results came out on 5 November 2026 and the interview schedule followed on 17 November. Personality tests run through January–April 2027, with the final result expected in April–May 2027. The next cycle — CSE 2027 — opens its notification on 13 January 2027.

The odds

One cycle, five numbers

CSE 2025, the last completed cycle. Nearly ten lakh people apply; about six in ten turn up; a little over fourteen thousand reach the Mains; fewer than a thousand make the final list.

Exam pattern

Three stages, nine written papers

Prelims is a screening test — its marks never carry forward. Everything you score in Mains and the interview is what decides your rank.

Stage I · Prelims

Two objective papers on one day, both of two hours. Only GS Paper I is counted for the cut-off; CSAT is a gate you must clear at 33%.

  • 200 marks counted
  • 100 + 80 questions
  • −⅓ negative marking

Role: screening only · result is pass or fail

Stage II · Mains

Nine descriptive papers across five days. Seven of them — Essay, GS I–IV and two optional papers — build the 1,750-mark merit list.

  • 1,750 marks counted
  • 7 merit papers
  • 10% floor per paper

Role: decides your rank · 250 marks a paper

Stage III · Interview

The Personality Test: a board of two or three members, about half an hour, on your DAF and the world around you. It is 275 marks — 13.6% of the final total.

  • 275 marks
  • 2–3 member board
  • ≈ 30 minutes

Role: moves ranks · a 40-mark swing is common

Where the 2,025 marks sit

Final merit = Mains written (1,750) + Personality Test (275)

Prelims cut-off counts GS-I only

CSAT is qualifying at 33% — 66 of 200 marks. Clear it and your CSAT score disappears from the calculation; miss it and even a 120 in GS-I is wasted.

Mains has a 10% floor per paper

Your aggregate may be well above the cut-off and still not count: UPSC requires at least 10% in each of the seven merit papers. One abandoned paper ends the run.

There is no normalisation

One paper, one sitting, one question paper — so UPSC's published cut-offs are raw marks you could actually have scored. No scaling, no shift-wise adjustment.

Why the two numbers differ

Every notice says the vacancy count "may undergo change after getting firm numbers from Cadre Controlling Authorities". Those firm numbers arrive more than a year later, with the final result.

The recommended list is usually shorter than the vacancies, and the gap is filled later from a consolidated reserve list — CSE 2020 recommended 761 against 836 vacancies and added 75 more from the reserve list in December 2021.

The IAS share has been fixed at 180 in every cycle from 2015 to 2025. Almost all the year-to-year movement sits in the Central Group A and Group B services.

Eligibility

Who can sit, and how often

Four numbers decide whether you can apply: your age, your degree, your category and how many attempts you have already spent.

Age

  • Minimum 21 years on 1 August of the exam year
  • General / EWS upper limit 32
  • OBC 35, SC/ST 37
  • PwBD candidates get +10 years on the upper limit

Attempts

  • General / EWS 6
  • OBC 9
  • SC / ST — no limit within the age band
  • An attempt counts only when you appear in Prelims

Qualification

  • A bachelor's degree in any stream from a recognised university
  • Final-year candidates may apply provisionally
  • No minimum percentage, no subject restriction
  • Medical standards apply to the IAS, IPS and IFS

Age relaxations also exist for ex-servicemen, persons domiciled in Jammu & Kashmir during the specified period, and certain other groups. Always read the age and attempt conditions in that year's official notice — they are the only ones that bind.

Where a rank can land you

All India Services — IAS, IPS, Indian Forest Service. State cadre, central deputation, constitutional role.

Group A central services — IRS (Income Tax), IRS (Customs & Indirect Taxes), Indian Audit & Accounts, Indian Civil Accounts, Corporate Law, Defence Accounts, Indian Information, Postal, the four Railway services, Trade Service.

Group B services — DANICS, Delhi Police Service, Puducherry Police Service, Armed Forces Headquarters Civil Service.

Allocation follows your rank and the preferences you file in DAF-II, so the order of that form matters as much as the rank itself.

The calendar

CSE 2027, start to finish

From UPSC's annual calendar released on 20 May 2026. A cycle runs about fifteen months from notification to final result.

  1. 13 January 2027 Notification CSE 2027 notice released; applications open on upsconline.nic.in
  2. 2 February 2027 Applications close Last date to submit the form, 6:00 PM
  3. 23 May 2027 Prelims GS Paper I in the morning session, CSAT in the afternoon
  4. 20 August 2027 Mains Nine papers across five days
  5. January–April 2028 Interview Personality tests at Dholpur House, New Delhi
  6. April–May 2028 Final result Rank list, service allocation and the reserve list

Dates move — plan for that

UPSC's calendar carries its own caveat: dates "are liable to alteration, if the circumstances so warrant". CSE 2021 was postponed by a Supreme Court case; CSE 2020 ran through the pandemic.

What has not moved in a decade is the shape: notification in January or early February, Prelims in late May, Mains in the second half of August or September, interviews from January, results by April–May.

Check upsc.gov.in for the current calendar before you block dates.

Nitty gritty

Twelve things worth knowing before you start

The rules that quietly decide outcomes — most of them are in the notice, and almost none of them are in the coaching brochure.

A cut-off is not a target

It is the last recommended candidate's score, published a year after the exam. Preparing to last year's number prepares you for last year's paper.

Negative marking is one-third

At 2 marks a question, three wrong answers cancel one right one. Blind elimination is a strategy only when the odds are better than one in four.

Prelims marks do not carry forward

Nothing you score in May survives into the final total. Clearing it is the whole job; the margin above the cut-off buys you nothing.

Attempts are counted, not earned

An attempt is spent the moment you appear in Prelims — including the one you wrote "just to see the paper". Skipping the exam costs nothing.

Two qualifying papers can end the run

The Indian language and English papers are 300 marks each and count for nothing — but scoring below 25% in either removes you from the list regardless of merit.

The optional is 500 marks

A quarter of the written total, and the only part of the syllabus entirely in your control. Syllabus overlap with GS and a scoring history beat prestige.

The interview is 275 marks

That is 13.6% of the final total — enough to move a candidate dozens of ranks either way. UPSC calls roughly 2.4 to 2.7 candidates per vacancy to it.

Notified vacancies are an estimate

The firm number comes with the final result, and it has been higher than the notice's figure in every year since 2015. Do not plan a cycle around the first number you see.

The list is shorter than the vacancies

UPSC recommends fewer candidates than there are posts and keeps a consolidated reserve list below the last recommended candidate in each category, used later.

Ties are broken in three steps

Higher total, then higher marks in the compulsory papers plus interview, then in the compulsory papers alone, and finally the older candidate ranks higher.

DAF-I and DAF-II are not paperwork

DAF-I follows Prelims and fixes your optional, medium and service preferences. DAF-II follows Mains and is where the interview board's questions begin.

Your medium is a strategy

You may write Mains in English or any scheduled language, and the optional must match. Marks are comparable across mediums, but preparation material is not equally available in all of them.
